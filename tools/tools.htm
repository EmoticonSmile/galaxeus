Building a bare-metal arcade game in pure x86 assembly means you can't just click "Export" in a modern game engine. To keep everything self-contained, fast, and clean, I wrote my own small toolchain to prepare the assets and build the bootable media for Galaxeus.

G-Slicer (Raw Floppy Disk Builder)

Galaxeus doesn't need DOS, FAT, or any filesystem overhead to run on real hardware. G-Slicer takes the compiled 64 KB binary and writes the custom boot sector and game code directly onto the raw sectors of a 1.44 MB floppy disk. Put the disk in, power on the 386, and the game boots straight off the metal.

ASCII-to-ASM Sprite Generator

Every single graphic in Galaxeus lives directly inside the assembly source as data definitions. Instead of wrestling with bloated external sprite editors or binary asset loaders, I built a custom converter that turns plain-text ASCII art drawings straight into MASM-ready .INC files. You can literally open a sprite in Notepad, tweak a few characters, re-run the tool, and assemble.

Digital Audio Sample Converter

FM synthesis never gave me the punchy, high-energy arcade feel I wanted - so Galaxeus uses real 11 kHz 8-bit digital PCM samples through the Sound Blaster. This utility strips away unnecessary headers and converts raw sound effects into clean DB byte sequences that MASM compiles straight into the executable.

Built-in Bootloader & Disk Cloner

Even the floppy bootloader itself doubles as a handy utility. When you exit the game on a floppy-booted machine, it drops right back into the loader menu, letting you tweak your Sound Blaster IRQ/DMA settings on the fly, boot over to a standard hard drive, or clone the entire game image onto a blank floppy disk without needing a single DOS utility.
